Summary

State-of-the-art hand mesh systems are benchmarked almost exclusively on FreiHAND — a Western-participant dataset. Korean hand morphology differs measurably: HaMeR's PA-MPJPE rises from 6.5mm (FreiHAND) to 8.14mm on Korean inputs — a 45% accuracy loss for any Korean deployment.

The naïve fix — fine-tuning HaMeR on Korean data — closes the gap to 5.78mm but causes +39% regression on FreiHAND and +48% on InterHand2.6M. Neither full fine-tuning (40M) nor LoRA (0.9M) avoids this: the trade-off is intrinsic to mano_head adaptation, not a parameter-budget problem.

Our approach: specialize additively, not by overwriting. A 22M-parameter specialist is trained and ensembled with frozen HaMeR via a learned per-rotation SLERP gating MLP. The gating defers to HaMeR on Western inputs (preserving accuracy) and blends the specialist in on Korean inputs. Result: PA-MPJPE 5.31mm, F@5mm 57.9% on Korean — beating full fine-tuning by 8.1% — with zero FreiHAND regression. What I trained is only 22.37M parameters; HaMeR's 632M backbone is unchanged.

Results

All results on a 3,000-frame, 15-subject, subject-disjoint Korean test partition. Across all slices evaluated — 25 grasp types, 27 object categories, 15 subjects, 4 viewpoints, 6 hand regions — the learned-α ensemble outperforms HaMeR-FT. Key Findings

1
The specialisation trade-off is intrinsic to mano_head adaptation. Both full fine-tuning (40M) and LoRA (0.9M) regress FreiHAND by +29–39%. The problem is the adaptation objective, not the parameter count.
2
Additive specialisation avoids the trade-off entirely. Keeping HaMeR frozen and training a separate small specialist preserves FreiHAND accuracy exactly while delivering best-in-class Korean performance across all 25 grasp types, 27 object categories, and 15 test subjects.
3
Population-specific MANO templates matter — independently of fine-tuning. The Korean template (fit to 4,532 scans, mean 4.31mm from standard MANO) contributes ~1.5mm PA-MPVPE improvement across all three predictor strengths. It is not a cosmetic preference.
4
The trade-off is shape-distribution-specific (+39%), not size-specific (+1%). Splitting AIHub by hand size (85mm vs 98mm subjects): a specialist trained on small-hand subjects regresses only +0.09mm on large-hand subjects — versus +39% to +48% for cross-ethnicity shifts. The 39× ratio localises the problem to population-level shape distribution, not arbitrary subgroups.
5
The gating MLP learns biomechanically meaningful α values without supervision. Wrist (joint 0): α ≈ 1.0 (defer to HaMeR's global rotation). Finger joints: α ≈ 0.55 (genuine blend). Per-image joint-spread of 0.14 confirms it is not collapsing to a global α.
6
Model disagreement is a free uncertainty signal. Specialist-vs-HaMeR rotation disagreement correlates with ensemble error at Pearson r = 0.50. The system can flag low-confidence predictions without any external supervision — deployment-relevant for clinical settings.

Limitations & Reviewer Questions

Honest limitations are discussed in the paper. The table below maps expected reviewer questions to the sections that address them.

Likely concern Defence in the paper
"Ensemble (694M) is heavier than HaMeR-FT (672M)" § Deployment recipes: specialist alone (22M, 7ms) is the light option; still 2.3mm better than HaMeR zero-shot
"Only +0.14mm from learned-α — worth it?" F@5mm +8.6pp, F@3mm +51% relative, AUC +3.8% — mean error understates the threshold-robust gain
"Single population — does it generalise?" Shape shift → +39%; size shift → +1%. The 39× ratio localises the tradeoff to shape distribution, not arbitrary subgroups
"What about LoRA / PEFT?" LoRA r=16 (0.9M params, 44× fewer than full FT) still regresses FreiHAND by +29% — tradeoff is intrinsic to mano_head objective
"Korean MANO template is cosmetic" 1.43–1.50mm PA-MPVPE penalty without it across three predictors; 4.31mm structured gap is not absorbable by PA alignment
"Is +5.14mm result statistically robust?" 5-fold subject-disjoint CV: +0.21 ± 0.02mm, no fold below +0.18mm; 25/25 grasps, 27/27 objects, 15/15 subjects, 4/4 viewpoints universal dominance

Technical Stack

Architecture
  • DINOv2-S (last 6 blocks fine-tuned)
  • SemGCN (21 landmarks)
  • Cross-attention fusion (2 layers)
  • IEF rotation head (6 steps, SO(3))
  • SLERP on SO(3)¹⁶
  • Gating MLP 453→512→256→16
Data & Training
  • AIHub Korean Hand (92K frames)
  • FreiHAND · HO-3D · DexYCB
  • Korean MANO template (4,532 scans)
  • AdamW · ~6h on RTX 4090
  • Geodesic + Frobenius rotation loss
  • HaMeR distillation (Procrustes L1)
Engineering
  • PyTorch · NumPy · OpenCV
  • Gradient checkpointing (bit-identical)
  • Resumable checkpointing
  • 5-fold cross-validation harness
  • Idle-GPU watcher + auto-launcher
  • MIT licence · full artifact release
